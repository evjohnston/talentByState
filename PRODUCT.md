# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Self-contained static HTML files (inline CSS/JS, data and fonts embedded), generated by Python:
`python -m ipeds_clean.states_explorer` writes `data/states/sheet.html` (the State Talent Sheet;
`explorer.html` redirects to it) and `python -m ipeds_clean.state_profile --all` writes the 51
`data/states/profiles/<ST>.html` pages plus `profiles/data/<ST>.json` for competitor overlays. Served
locally (`cd data/states && python -m http.server 8765`) and opened on localhost; never published as
claude.ai artifacts. No build step, no framework.

## Users

Primary (from 2026-11-04): U.S. state policymakers and their staff (governors' offices, legislators,
higher-education coordinating boards, workforce and economic-development agencies). They open their
own state to see its talent ecosystem (students, degrees, international and STEM talent, prices,
outcomes, migration), how it has changed over time, and how it stands against the states they see
as competitors. Secondary: the researcher who built it (co-author of the 2026 US Talent Report),
finding stories and verifying numbers.

## Product Purpose

Answer a policymaker's three questions for their state, in this order: where do we stand now, how
did we get here (trend), and how do we compare with our competitors (chosen peer states and the
U.S.). Success = a staffer can find their state's position and trajectory on a talent measure, see
which competitor is ahead, and cite the number, in minutes. The state explorer (all states on one
measure) and the 51 state profiles (one state, every measure) serve this together.

## Positioning

Built on this project's own harmonized IPEDS panels, so every number reconciles with the national
totals the report publishes (Table 1.01 international shares within 0.8 pp).

## Operating Context

Desktop browser during analysis sessions, next to the Python/IPEDS project and the report draft.
Findings are later re-made as static PNG/SVG figures (`data/states/figures/`) or report charts in R.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Data: ~100 measures x 51 states (+ US) x 1980–2025 (coverage varies by measure), from
  `ipeds_clean.states.MEASURES` (title, unit, format).
- Geography = institution location; international = US nonresidents; STEM = CIP families
  03, 04, 11, 14, 15, 26, 27, 40.
- Known caveats that must stay visible: online mega-universities (WGU/UT, UMGC/MD, SNHU/NH) dominate
  some states; Colorado appropriations; 2024 data partly provisional; 1981–83 absent.
- Sources: IPEDS is the backbone; DHS SEVIS by the Numbers (sevisdata/, parsed by ipeds_clean/sevis.py) adds country of citizenship and STEM-by-level counts. It is one story, not an IPEDS section and a non-IPEDS section: every figure carries its own source line ("Data from NCES, IPEDS" or "Data from DHS, SEVIS by the Numbers"). The one exception is grouping: peer-set presets may use outside public state attributes (state GDP and population via FRED, 2024 presidential vote from MIT Election Lab, Census divisions) only to choose which states to compare, and say so in the menu.

## Brand Commitments

Neutral research tool: the interactive explorer and profiles carry no Hoover, Stanford, HAI, or Talent Report branding. Exception (user request, 2026-11-05): the PDF State Talent Profiles are companions to the 2026 US Talent Report and carry the Hoover|Stanford and HAI logos (reference/logo/) and the report's cover and front-matter style.

## Evidence on Hand

`data/states/state_panel.csv`, `ipeds_clean/states.py` (measure definitions, tile layout, notes),
static maps in `data/states/figures/{png,svg}/`.

## Product Principles

1. Every number is traceable: show definition, unit, year, and caveats next to the value.
2. Comparison first: all 51 states at once, change over time one click away.
3. Never imply precision the data don't have (small cells, provisional years, online institutions).
4. Fast to scan, fast to verify.
5. Peers are first-class: every state view can be read against chosen competitor states, not only the U.S.
6. Data forward: numbers and charts lead, chrome and prose recede. Every figure can be re-pointed on the spot (state, competitor set, year, degree level, measure) without leaving the page.
